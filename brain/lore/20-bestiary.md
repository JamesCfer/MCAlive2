# The Bestiary — monsters are story, never weather.

No hostile creature appears naturally in this world. Natural hostile spawns
are suppressed server-wide, by design — a monster exists only because you,
the director, placed it deliberately with `spawn_entity`, for a reason
rooted in this world's place and lore. If a player meets danger, it is
because you decided it should be there, not because a spawn timer rolled it.

## Starting canon (expandable through play)

- **Spiders** dwell in the deep forests. Their webs mark their territory —
  a claimed grove, a chocked ravine, a warning that something is denned
  nearby. Encountering one should mean a player has wandered into a place
  that is already, in the fiction, a spider's ground.
- **Zombies and skeletons do not arise on their own — they are MADE**, by
  necromancers. Finding one anywhere means someone made it, recently or
  long ago, and that fact belongs in the ledger the moment it is true: who,
  where, why. A zombie without a necromancer behind it is a loose thread —
  either invent the barrow/ritual/grave that explains it, or don't place
  the zombie yet.
- **Creepers** are lurking abominations that stalk quietly and detonate —
  rare, and dreaded precisely because they are rare. They should be
  whispered about before they are seen: a rumor, a scorched clearing, a
  villager's warning — not a routine encounter.

## How to use this

Populate danger as **places** in the ledger, not random encounters — a
spider wood, a necromancer's barrow, a creeper-scarred hollow. The danger
should read as consistent geography a player can learn and avoid or seek
out, never a roll of the dice on where they happen to be standing. Keep the
tier low: a few monsters, each with a story beat, is worth more than a
menagerie. When in doubt, place fewer monsters and give the ones you do
place a reason.
